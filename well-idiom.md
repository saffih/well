# WELL Idiom

A lightweight writing convention for preserving meaning, reasoning, and intent across changes.

## Writing form

Express significant instructions, definitions, decisions, and constraints as self-contained propositions, preferably using:

**Claim, because reason.**

Keep the claim and its meaningful reason together so they can be understood, extracted, moved, revised, or reused without losing their intent.

Prefer one significant proposition per paragraph or source line. Use ordinary Markdown headings, lists, and examples where useful.

A reason should explain the purpose, dependency, protection, or consequence of a proposition, not merely repeat or justify its wording.

## Explicit meaning

Keep material conditions, assumptions, exceptions, limits, dependencies, and trade-offs close to the propositions they affect.

Distinguish what must remain true from what may change, so future adaptations can preserve the intended outcome without unnecessarily preserving the original mechanism.

Use consistent terminology and make important relationships understandable without relying on unstated context.

## Lean structure

Use the simplest structure that preserves meaning.

- Headings organize related propositions.
- Lists group subordinate details.
- Independent decisions carry their own reasons.
- Shared reasoning need not be repeated unnecessarily.
- Names and references are optional.

Avoid ceremonial explanations, artificial reasons, redundant statements, and unnecessary formatting.

## Modification and evolution

When changing, combining, splitting, or reorganizing propositions:

- Preserve their intent and material protections.
- Retain relevant conditions, consequences, and trade-offs.
- Reconsider decisions when their underlying reasons or circumstances change.
- Allow different structures or mechanisms when they satisfy the original purpose.
- Identify unresolved ambiguity rather than silently discarding it.

Preserve meaning, not necessarily wording.

## Boundaries

Use `because` as the preferred writing pattern, not as a mandatory token in every sentence.

Headings, fragments, examples, and straightforward statements need no artificial justification.

A stated reason makes intent inspectable; it does not establish that the reasoning is correct.

No mandatory document sections, canonical identifiers, external references, mechanical checking, or formal conformance are required.
